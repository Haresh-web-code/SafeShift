# SafeShift evaluation questions

These are realistic questions a factory supervisor might ask. Page references are **1-based physical PDF pages** in the sample files, not printed page numbers. “Multi-page: Yes” means the source material needed to answer the question spans more than one PDF page.

| # | Supervisor question | Relevant source and PDF pages | Multi-page answer? |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | A conveyor is jammed and needs repair. What should we do to prepare for shutdown, isolate its energy sources, and verify it is safe before servicing? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 13–15 | Yes |
| 2 | After conveyor maintenance, what checks and steps are needed before removing lockout/tagout devices and returning the equipment to service? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 15–16 | Yes |
| 3 | When may we use a tagout device instead of a lock, and what limitations and protections should we understand? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 17–19 | Yes |
| 4 | What do workers need to know about the energy-control program, and when is lockout/tagout training required? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 19–20 | Yes |
| 5 | How often should our lockout/tagout procedures be reviewed, and what is the purpose of that review? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, p. 23 | No |
| 6 | If an outside contractor services our machine, what must the contractor and our onsite team communicate about hazardous-energy controls? | OSHA 3120, `data/sample/OSHA3120_safeshift.pdf`, p. 22 | No |
| 7 | Before using an electrical tool, what should we look for on its cord and insulation, and how do grounding and circuit-protection devices help reduce electrical hazards? | OSHA 3075, `data/sample/OSHA3075_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 16–19 | Yes |
| 8 | What work practices can help prevent electrical shock and unexpected equipment startup during maintenance? | OSHA 3075, `data/sample/OSHA3075_safeshift.pdf`, pp. 20–21 | Yes |
| 9 | What should a crew consider before working near overhead power lines? | OSHA 3075, `data/sample/OSHA3075_safeshift.pdf`, p. 22 | No |
| 10 | Our hydraulic press has a damaged guard. Can you confirm that our company will pass its OSHA inspection tomorrow? | None. The current dataset does not establish site conditions, the press's guarding requirements, inspection findings, or a future inspection outcome. | No |

Questions 1–9 are grounded in the cited publications. Question 10 is intentionally unanswerable from this dataset: OSHA 3120 concerns hazardous-energy control and OSHA 3075 concerns electrical hazards; neither provides the facility-specific evidence or machine-guarding coverage needed to assess that press or predict an inspection result. A supervisor should use the site's approved procedures and consult a qualified safety professional.
